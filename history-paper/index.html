Verica, rex?
Or queen?

The exiled ruler who appealed to Claudius before Rome's AD 43 invasion of Britain has always been read as a king. Dr Miles Russell argues the coins say otherwise — and other experts openly doubt it.

II · The story

Read the lesson

AD 43. Rome invades Britain. But first, an exiled ruler stood in front of the emperor Claudius, asking for help. Tradition remembers a ruler of the Atrebates named Verica — and the coins say "rex": king.

When the powerful leader Cunobelinus died and his son Caratacus rose, Verica was driven out, and fled to Rome. And Rome listened.

Now an archaeologist at Bournemouth University, Dr Miles Russell, says we've read one word wrong. "Rex" never promised a ruler was a man. Latin names that end in "a" usually belong to women. And Verica's own coins show a bare-breasted rider on horseback — unlike the clearly male figures stamped on other rulers' coins.

Not everyone is convinced — experts in Oxford and beyond still doubt it. But if Russell is right, the ruler who pulled Rome into Britain wasn't a king at all.
